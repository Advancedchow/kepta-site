Holiday Leftovers: How Long They Last

From the Christmas ham to the last of the latkes, most holiday leftovers keep 3 to 4 days in the refrigerator. Get them there within 2 hours, and reheat them to 165°F before eating.

Every food safety number on this page comes from the USDA and FoodSafety.gov, including USDA's FoodKeeper data, linked at the end of the page.

3 to 4 days

How long most leftovers keep in the refrigerator, the roast and the latkes included.

2 hours

The longest perishable food should sit out. Make it 1 hour if the room is above 90°F.

165°F

The temperature to reheat leftovers to, checked with a food thermometer.

How long each dish keeps

Freezer times are for best quality: frozen food stays safe indefinitely but loses moisture and flavor the longer it waits.

Buffets and party platters

Perishable food that has been out more than 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F, should be thrown away. A long, grazing party gets there faster than it feels.

  • ●Keep hot food at 140°F or warmer in chafing dishes, slow cookers, or warming trays.
  • ●Keep cold food at 40°F or below: nest the dish in a bowl of ice, or set out small trays and replace them often.
  • ●USDA's own example of cold perishable food is a platter of deli meats. The shrimp cocktail beside it needs the same care.

Cooling and storing

Bacteria grow rapidly between 40°F and 140°F, so the goal is to get food cold quickly. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or 1 hour if it's above 90°F.

  • ●Slice or cut a whole roast or ham into smaller parts so it cools fast.
  • ●Divide big batches, like a pot of soup, into shallow containers.
  • ●Cut the turkey into smaller pieces and slice the breast meat. Legs and wings can stay whole.
  • ●Hot food can go straight into the refrigerator, or into an ice or cold water bath first.
  • ●Cover leftovers, wrap them airtight, or seal them in containers.

Freezing and thawing

Freezer times in the chart are for best quality. When you want something back, there are three safe ways to thaw:

  • ●In the refrigerator. Use within 3 to 4 days, or refreeze. From a large container thawed this way, you can take a portion and refreeze the rest without reheating.
  • ●In cold water, in a leak-proof bag. Cook it before refreezing.
  • ●In the microwave. Heat to 165°F, and then it can be refrozen.

You can also reheat frozen leftovers without thawing them first.

Reheating

Reheat leftovers to 165°F, measured with a food thermometer. Bring sauces, soups, and gravies to a rolling boil, and cover leftovers while they reheat.

  • ●In the microwave, cover the dish, rotate it, and add a little liquid if it needs it.
  • ●Vent the cover, then check the temperature in several places, because microwaves leave cold spots.
  • ●Let it rest before serving.
  • ●Food left over after reheating to 165°F is safe to refreeze.

A calm plan for the holiday weekends

Christmas Day and New Year's Day both land on a Friday this year, so the same plan works twice. Count the day the meal is cooked as day 1.

Friday night, day 1

Before the kitchen closes, cut the roast or ham into smaller pieces, portion everything into shallow containers, and label each one with the date. Anything that sat out more than 2 hours goes, without guilt.

Saturday, day 2

Be honest about what you'll actually eat. Freeze the rest now, while it still has its best texture, but keep the custard and cream pies, the salads, and the cranberry sauce out of the freezer: they don't freeze well.

Sunday and Monday, days 3 and 4

Finish what's left in the fridge or toss it, by Monday, December 28, or Monday, January 4. The fruit pie, the opened hard cheese, and the homemade applesauce can wait longer. Most of the plate can't.

Eight nights of Hanukkah

Hanukkah runs from sundown on Friday, December 4, through Saturday, December 12, so latkes and brisket tend to arrive in batches. Give each batch its own date and its own 3 to 4 days.
